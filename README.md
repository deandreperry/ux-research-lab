# UX Research Lab

A collection of UX research work focused on accessible products, developer tools, learning experiences, and nonprofit websites.

## About Me

I'm DeAndre Perry. My background sits between UX research, product thinking, accessibility, and frontend development. I enjoy taking a broad product question, figuring out what the team actually needs to learn, and turning that research into clear next steps.

I built this repository to document how I approach that work. It includes research plans, interview guides, surveys, usability studies, synthesis tools, and experiment plans. You can find more of my work on [GitHub](https://github.com/deandreperry).

## A Note About The Work

Most of these projects are simulations. They show how I would plan and carry out the research, but they do not claim that interviews or usability sessions already happened.

I label assumptions as assumptions and keep example findings separate from real evidence. When I add completed research in the future, I will only share participant material that is de-identified and approved for public use. Recordings, contact details, and private client information do not belong in this repository.

## How The Pieces Connect

A project starts with a decision the team needs to make. The plan explains what we need to learn and why a method fits. The research tools collect the evidence. Synthesis turns that evidence into findings, and the decision log shows what changed afterward.

I use a few simple labels throughout the repository:

- **Evidence:** something observed or measured
- **Interpretation:** what I think the evidence may mean
- **Hypothesis:** an idea that still needs to be tested
- **Decision:** what the team chooses to do and why

## Working Principles

- Be honest about what is real, simulated, or still unknown.
- Choose a method because it fits the question, not just to check a box.
- Keep observations separate from interpretation.
- Look for evidence that challenges the preferred solution.
- Include accessibility, consent, privacy, and participant comfort in the plan from the start.
- Connect recommendations to an owner and a way to check whether they worked.
- Write for people who need to make a decision, not only for other researchers.
