# Nonprofit Website: Balancing Donor Confidence And Staff Capacity

- **Status:** Planning only; the study has not been run
- **My role:** Research plan, content and journey review, interview and test design, and experiment planning
- **Product stage:** Redesign discovery

## What I Want To Learn

I want to learn which website changes would help first-time visitors understand and trust a small nonprofit while staying realistic for the staff members who maintain the site.

## The People Involved

- Donors need credible, specific information without pressure or exaggerated impact claims.
- Volunteers need clear requirements, commitment, and next steps.
- Program participants may have different privacy and accessibility needs than donors.
- Staff need content structures they can maintain with limited time and technical support.
- Leadership needs evidence that recommendations support mission and operational goals.

I would study these groups separately. Sharing the same website does not mean they have the same goal.

## How I Would Research It

1. Interview staff about content operations, approvals, recurring questions, and measurement.
2. Audit key pages for mission clarity, accessibility, privacy, and current content.
3. Observe first-time donors and prospective volunteers completing separate task flows.
4. Synthesize needs by intent rather than treating all visitors as one segment.
5. Test revised content and navigation before considering a live experiment.

## Experiment Boundaries

- Validate that donation-impact claims are accurate and appropriately qualified.
- Do not use urgency, guilt, or confusing defaults to increase conversion.
- Treat accessibility, privacy, and payment errors as guardrails.
- Define assignment, instrumentation, minimum detectable effect, and stopping rules before launch.
- Pair conversion measures with donor comprehension and support indicators.

## What I Would Measure Later

The research should help the organization choose a manageable content structure and clearer donation and volunteer paths. If changes are published, I would measure mission understanding, task success, form errors, completion, support requests, and the time staff spend maintaining content. Fundraising can change for many reasons, so I would not credit one interface change without stronger evidence.
