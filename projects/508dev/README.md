# 508dev: From Accessibility Detection To Team Action

- **Status:** Planning only; the study has not been run
- **My role:** Research plan, interview and test design, synthesis plan, and communication
- **Product stage:** Concept discovery

## What I Want To Learn

I want to learn what an audit workflow needs to help teams understand an accessibility issue, verify it, decide who owns it, and move toward a fix. Automated checks can be useful, but they cannot be the whole answer.

## Why It Matters

When accessibility work happens late, disabled people face barriers and product teams face avoidable rework. I would include disabled participants as paid experts in their own experience without expecting any one person to speak for a whole community.

## Current Hypotheses

- Teams may need user-impact explanations before technical guidance becomes meaningful.
- Separating impact, frequency, fix effort, and release risk may support clearer prioritization.
- Role-specific views may help ownership while a shared evidence model preserves alignment.
- Automated findings must be paired with guided manual and assistive-technology validation.

These are starting assumptions. I would expect the research to change some of them.

## How I Would Research It

1. Review standards, existing audit workflows, and known limitations of automation.
2. Interview product-team members about recent accessibility decisions.
3. Conduct compensated sessions with disabled participants using an accessible prototype.
4. Synthesize product-team and lived-experience evidence without collapsing them into one perspective.
5. Test a revised audit-to-ticket workflow with both groups.

## How I Would Work With Participants

- Recruit for relevant access experiences, not a generic “accessibility user.”
- Ask about accommodations before the session and provide materials in advance.
- Support participant-selected assistive technology and communication methods.
- Pay participants for their time and expertise, including pilots.
- Do not publish disability details or quotations without explicit permission.
- Document which experiences are absent from the sample.
- Involve accessibility specialists in protocol and recommendation review.

## What I Would Measure Later

The first result should be a clear choice about the workflow to prototype. If the product is eventually built, I would look at manual-test completion, ticket quality, time from finding an issue to verifying the fix, repeated accessibility defects, and feedback from disabled users. I would establish a baseline before calling any change an improvement.
