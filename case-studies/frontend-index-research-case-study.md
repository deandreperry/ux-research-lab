# Frontend Index Research Case Study

- **Status:** Study ready; research has not started
- **My role:** Research plan, interview and test design, synthesis plan, and communication
- **Decision:** Select the information architecture and content model to prototype before product investment

## Why This Project

Frontend Index is a proposed reference hub for UI patterns, accessibility behavior, and implementation guidance. Designers and engineers frequently move between design systems, code documentation, search results, and accessibility references before deciding whether a pattern is appropriate.

The hard part is not finding an example. It is deciding whether that example fits the problem. Incomplete guidance can lead to rework, inconsistent patterns, and accessibility mistakes.

## How I Would Approach It

I would spend four weeks reviewing existing systems, interviewing six practitioners about recent decisions, and testing the information architecture with five people. Interviews help me understand how the decision works today. Task testing lets me see whether the proposed structure actually helps.

The group would include designers and frontend or design-system engineers who made a pattern decision within the last three months. The sample is meant to show behavior and variation, not estimate how common something is across the industry.

## Hypotheses To Test

- Practitioners may begin with a user goal rather than an internal component name.
- Trust may depend on rationale, states, accessibility behavior, implementation constraints, and evidence that guidance is maintained.
- Designers and developers may share a high-level decision path but need different implementation depth.
- Accessibility information may be missed when separated from the main pattern guidance.

These ideas give the study something to challenge. They are not findings.

## How I Would Handle The Evidence

I would connect every note to a research question, code the sessions with a documented codebook, and keep examples that contradict the main pattern. Each finding would link back to evidence and explain the limits, confidence, recommendation, owner, and next check.

## What It Would Help Decide

The research would help the team decide whether to use task-based navigation, a consistent content structure, role-specific detail, and side-by-side comparison. The decision log would show what the team accepted, tested, delayed, or declined.

If the product is built, I would later track task success, correct use-case understanding, accessibility-guidance discovery, failed searches, clarification requests, and pattern-related defects. There is no outcome to report yet.

## Reflection

My frontend background helps me notice implementation constraints, but it could also make me give developer needs too much weight. I would include design participants, look for opposing evidence, and ask another researcher to review the synthesis.
