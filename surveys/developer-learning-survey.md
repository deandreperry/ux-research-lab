# Developer Learning Survey

**Artifact type:** Portfolio Simulation

## Survey Goal

Measure how learners discover, evaluate, and retain developer education content, especially for command-line and frontend workflows.

## Target Audience

Beginner to intermediate developers, bootcamp students, self-taught learners, and designers learning technical implementation.

## Screening Questions

- Which best describes your current experience level?
- Have you used the command line in the past 30 days?
- Have you completed a frontend tutorial in the past six months?

## Multiple-Choice Questions

- What type of learning resource do you use most often?
  - Official documentation
  - Video tutorials
  - Interactive lessons
  - Blog posts
  - Peer support or community forums
- What usually causes you to abandon a lesson?
  - Unclear prerequisites
  - Setup errors
  - Too much theory
  - Too little explanation
  - Outdated code

## Likert Scale Questions

Rate from 1, strongly disagree, to 5, strongly agree:

- I feel confident recovering from command-line errors.
- I understand why commands work, not just what to type.
- Learning materials usually explain prerequisites clearly.
- Interactive practice helps me remember technical concepts.

## Open-Ended Questions

- Describe a recent learning moment that felt frustrating.
- What makes a technical lesson feel trustworthy?
- What feedback would help you recover from mistakes faster?

## How Results Would Be Analyzed

Responses would be segmented by experience level and command-line confidence. Quantitative results would identify confidence gaps and abandonment drivers. Open-ended responses would be coded into themes such as setup friction, unclear mental models, error recovery, pacing, and trust.
